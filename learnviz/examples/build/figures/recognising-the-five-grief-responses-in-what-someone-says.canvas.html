Image Description

  • Main subject/content: Recognising the five grief responses in what someone says. A comparison table with 5 criteria down the left and 3 items across the top.

    • Denial: What it can sound like, There has been a mistake. Get the tests done again. What the person may need, Time, and the information offered again later rather than pressed now. What not to say, You need to accept this.
    • Anger: What it can sound like, Why did nobody pick this up sooner? Often aimed at whoever is in the room. What the person may need, To be heard without the anger being taken personally or argued down. What not to say, There is no point getting angry about it.
    • Bargaining: What it can sound like, If I do everything right, will that change it? Often carries what if and if only. What the person may need, Honesty about what is and is not possible, given without stripping out hope. What not to say, That is not going to make any difference.
    • Depression: What it can sound like, Little talk. Withdrawal from things that used to matter. What the person may need, Someone present rather than fixing it. Watch for risk and escalate to a supervisor. What not to say, Try to look on the bright side.
    • Acceptance: What it can sound like, I want to get the house sorted out. Practical planning. What the person may need, Support to act on the decisions they have made themselves. What not to say, You are being so brave about it.

  • Important visual details: Column headers are filled in a different colour per item, with white text. Rows alternate between white and light grey.

  • Relevant structure such as columns, layers, or groupings: 5 rows, one per criterion. 3 columns, one per item: What it can sound like, What the person may need, What not to say. Read across a row to compare all items on one criterion, or down a column to see one item in full.

  • Colours/layout: Drawn in the RMIT palette on a white background, with navy #000054 text. Accent colours in use: navy, teal, magenta, ochre, blue. No information is carried by colour alone. Every coloured element also carries a number, a label or a texture, so nothing is lost in greyscale or by a learner with colour vision deficiency. These are responses, not stages in a fixed order. A person can move between them, return to one they seemed to have left, or never show some of them at all. The order of the rows here is the order Kübler-Ross listed them in, and nothing more. Treat a person who is angry in week one and angry again in week six as normal, not as having gone backwards.
