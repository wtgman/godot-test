What was Instagram earning?

Facebook agreed to pay about US$1 billion for Instagram in April 2012. Before you look it up, decide how much revenue you think Instagram was making.

After this, you can explain what a buyer is paying for when a company has no revenue at all.

When Facebook agreed to pay about US$1 billion for Instagram, how much revenue was Instagram making?

When Facebook agreed to pay about US$1 billion for Instagram, how much revenue was Instagram making?

Make your prediction first. The point is to commit before you see the answer.

Nothing. So what was Facebook buying?

Instagram had no revenue, no advertising and thirteen staff. What Facebook paid for was about 30 million users, a team that had built something people used every day, and the certainty that a competitor would not own it.

  • When a company has no revenue, its price is a bet about the future, not a calculation about the present.
  • Buying a company partly to stop a rival buying it is a strategy with a name: a defensive acquisition.
Text version of this activity
What was Instagram earning?
===========================

What this shows: A prediction activity. The learner answers a question before the answer is shown, then sees the answer and the reasoning.
How it is arranged: Question first, then the learner's answer, then the reveal.

Contents:
  - Question: When Facebook agreed to pay about US$1 billion for Instagram, how much revenue was Instagram making?
  - Option 1: Nothing at all. Right. Instagram had never charged anyone for anything, and ran no advertising.
  - Option 2: About US$10 million a year. A reasonable guess for a popular app, but Instagram had no revenue at all.
  - Option 3: About US$100 million a year. That would put the price at ten times revenue, a common rule of thumb. The real answer makes the rule of thumb useless.
  - Option 4: About US$1 billion a year. That would make the deal look cheap. In fact Instagram earned nothing.
  - Nothing. So what was Facebook buying?: Nothing at all.
  - Why: Instagram had no revenue, no advertising and thirteen staff. What Facebook paid for was about 30 million users, a team that had built something people used every day, and the certainty that a competitor would not own it.
  - Point 1: When a company has no revenue, its price is a bet about the future, not a calculation about the present.
  - Point 2: Buying a company partly to stop a rival buying it is a strategy with a name: a defensive acquisition.
