Explore: Pricing a dish from its food cost

 

How it works.

  • Menu price before GST. Portion cost divided by the target food cost percentage.
  • Price the customer pays. The menu price with 10 per cent GST added.
  • Gross profit per plate. What is left of the price before GST once the ingredients are paid for.

Challenge 1: Your supplier puts the portion cost up to $11. Keep food cost at 30 per cent. What does the menu price before GST need to be?

Hint. Set the portion cost to $11 and the target back to 30 per cent.

What you should find. About $36.67. A $2 rise in cost needs a price rise of about $6.67 to hold the same percentage, because the price has to cover the cost more than three times over.

 

Challenge 2: A competitor sells the same dish for $32 before GST. At a portion cost of $11, roughly what food cost percentage would you be running if you matched them?

Hint. Leave the cost at $11 and move the target until the price is close to $32.

What you should find. About 34 per cent. Matching the competitor's price costs you around four points of margin on every plate.

 

Challenge 3: Find a portion cost and a target that give a price before GST of exactly $40. What does the customer pay?

Hint. Try $12 at 30 per cent, or $10 at 25 per cent.

What you should find. $44. GST goes on top of the price you set, so work the price out first and then add 10 per cent.
