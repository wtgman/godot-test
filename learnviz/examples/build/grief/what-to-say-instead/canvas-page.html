Recall practice: What to say instead

 

What could you say instead?

Say or write your answer first. Opening the card before you have tried is reading, not practising.

 

"You need to accept this."

"This is a lot to take in." It acknowledges the shock without telling them what they should be feeling.

 

"At least he had a long life."

"Tell me about him." Any sentence that starts with "at least" measures their loss for them.

 

"I know how you feel."

"I can't imagine what this is like for you." Nobody knows exactly, and saying so invites them to tell you.

 

"Everything happens for a reason."

"I'm so sorry." Simple is almost always better than an explanation.

 

"You should be over this by now."

"How are you going with it all?" Grief keeps no timetable, and a check-in is better than a deadline.

 

"Stay strong for the family."

"It's alright not to be okay." Asking someone to be strong asks them to hide.

 

"There's no point getting angry."

"I can hear how angry you are." Naming the anger lets it be said, which usually lowers it.
