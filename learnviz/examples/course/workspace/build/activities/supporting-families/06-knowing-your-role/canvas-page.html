Put it in order: Passing on a concern

 

A family member tells you something that worries you. Put these steps in the order you would take them.

These steps are out of order. Write down the order you think is right, then open the box to check.

  • Acknowledge what they have said
  • Tell them you will pass it on, and to whom
  • Tell your supervisor the same shift, in person
  • Listen without interrupting
  • Record it in the progress notes
  • Check back with the family that someone has followed up

 

Check the order
  1. Listen without interrupting. You cannot pass on accurately what you did not hear in full.
  2. Acknowledge what they have said. It tells them they were heard before anything else happens.
  3. Tell them you will pass it on, and to whom. They should never be surprised that someone else knows.
  4. Tell your supervisor the same shift, in person. A note alone can sit unread. In person, the same shift, it gets acted on.
  5. Record it in the progress notes. The record comes after the conversation with your supervisor, as your service requires.
  6. Check back with the family that someone has followed up. Closing the loop is what the family remembers, and it catches the follow-ups that did not happen.
