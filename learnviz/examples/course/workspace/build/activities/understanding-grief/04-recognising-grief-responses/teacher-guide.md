# Which response is this?

> **Draft.** 2 sources still to check. Check it with the review sheet before students see it.

- **Activity.** Sort it. The learner classifies real examples and is told why each belongs where it does.
- **Learning intent.** Learners can recognise each of the five responses from what a family member actually says.
- **Best placed.** After class, as practice or revision
- **Why it works.** Recognition is learned from contrasting examples, not from definitions, and the reason given for each item teaches the cue to look for.
- **In Canvas.** Through the SCORM package, it sends completion and a score to Canvas. The score kept is the learner's best attempt, so trying again never lowers it, and showing a model answer never raises it. A pass needs 70 per cent.

## Using it across the week

- **Before class.** Can work as a before-class diagnostic: the score tells you which categories the group already confuses.
- **During class.** In presenter view, the right arrow moves through the examples one at a time. Read each one out, ask the room to vote on the category, then press R to show the answer and the reason. The disagreements are where the teaching is.
- **After class.** Set it as practice after the topic. Learners can retry the ones they missed until the cues are secure.

### Listen for

Learners who treat the sort as a diagnosis. It is practice in noticing, not in labelling people. On placement, nobody should be told which stage they are in.

### Questions to ask the room

1. Which two were hardest to tell apart? What was the cue that settled it?
2. Bargaining sounds hopeful. Why might that make it easy to miss?

## Answer key

| Prompt | Answer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "The scan must have been someone else's. These things get mixed up all the time." | Denial | It rejects the information itself. The cue is disbelief about the facts, rather than distress about them. |
| "Why did it take three months for anyone to send him for a scan?" | Anger | The blame points outward, at the system. It accepts the diagnosis and is furious about how it arrived. |
| "If I get him walking every day and off the sugar, that will buy him more time, won't it?" | Bargaining | A deal: if I do this, then that. It sounds practical and hopeful, which is exactly why it is easy to miss. |
| "I don't see the point in getting dressed any more." | Depression | Loss of meaning in ordinary things. Listen, and pass it on to your supervisor. This is where you watch for risk. |
| "I want to sit down with the kids this weekend and talk about what he would want." | Acceptance | Planning around the reality rather than against it. Acceptance is not happiness. It is engaging with what is. |
| "You people are useless. Nobody tells us anything." | Anger | Aimed at the person in the room. It is almost never about that person, so it is worth not taking it personally. |
| "I keep thinking if I had made him go to the doctor sooner, none of this would be happening." | Bargaining | "If only" looks backwards, but it is the same move as a deal: searching for the lever that would have changed the outcome. It often comes with guilt. |
| "He's fine. He was up and about this morning. He just needs a good rest." | Denial | The evidence of the morning is used to push away the diagnosis. Denial often protects someone while the news sinks in. |
| "I haven't been sleeping. I just sit in his room." | Depression | Withdrawal, and a sign of how much this is costing them. Worth mentioning to the team so someone checks on the family as well as the resident. |

## Getting it into Canvas

Four routes, from the most capable to the most portable. Choose one for students, and keep presenter view for class.

### 1. As a SCORM activity (tracked, can be graded)

Upload `recognising-grief-responses.scorm.zip` through the SCORM tool in your course navigation, if your institution has it switched on. Choose whether to import it as a graded assignment. The activity runs inside Canvas and reports back.

### 2. As a Canvas page (works everywhere, no hosting)

Open `canvas-page.html` in a text editor, copy everything, and paste it into the HTML editor of a Canvas page. It uses Emble blocks and expandable answers rather than scripts, so it survives the Canvas editor and works in the mobile app. Learners answer before they open each answer.

### 3. As an embedded web page

If your institution can host a web page, publish `index.html` and paste `canvas-embed.html` into a Canvas page, replacing the highlighted placeholder with the page address. Do not upload `index.html` to Canvas Files: Canvas shows uploaded HTML without running its scripts, so it will load and do nothing.

### 4. In class, from your own computer

Open `index.html` in a browser and add `#present` to the end of the address, or use the Presenter view link at the bottom of the page. It needs no internet connection and no login.
