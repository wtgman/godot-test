Sort it: Which response is this?

 

Which response does each of these show most clearly?

Decide which it is for each example before you open it. The answer and the reason are inside.

  • Denial. Not accepting the information itself.
  • Anger. Blame, often aimed at whoever is in the room.
  • Bargaining. If I do this, then that. Or if only.
  • Depression. Withdrawal and loss of meaning.
  • Acceptance. Planning around what is real.

 

1. "I don't see the point in getting dressed any more."

Depression. Loss of meaning in ordinary things. Listen, and pass it on to your supervisor. This is where you watch for risk.

 

2. "I want to sit down with the kids this weekend and talk about what he would want."

Acceptance. Planning around the reality rather than against it. Acceptance is not happiness. It is engaging with what is.

 

3. "Why did it take three months for anyone to send him for a scan?"

Anger. The blame points outward, at the system. It accepts the diagnosis and is furious about how it arrived.

 

4. "If I get him walking every day and off the sugar, that will buy him more time, won't it?"

Bargaining. A deal: if I do this, then that. It sounds practical and hopeful, which is exactly why it is easy to miss.

 

5. "The scan must have been someone else's. These things get mixed up all the time."

Denial. It rejects the information itself. The cue is disbelief about the facts, rather than distress about them.

 

6. "You people are useless. Nobody tells us anything."

Anger. Aimed at the person in the room. It is almost never about that person, so it is worth not taking it personally.

 

7. "I keep thinking if I had made him go to the doctor sooner, none of this would be happening."

Bargaining. "If only" looks backwards, but it is the same move as a deal: searching for the lever that would have changed the outcome. It often comes with guilt.

 

8. "I haven't been sleeping. I just sit in his room."

Depression. Withdrawal, and a sign of how much this is costing them. Worth mentioning to the team so someone checks on the family as well as the resident.

 

9. "He's fine. He was up and about this morning. He just needs a good rest."

Denial. The evidence of the morning is used to push away the diagnosis. Denial often protects someone while the news sinks in.
