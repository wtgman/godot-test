Predict: Put the five stages of grief in order

 

Before you read on, decide your answer. Write it down or say it out loud. Then open the box below to check.

In what order do people move through these five stages of grief?

Put these in order:

  • Denial
  • Bargaining
  • Acceptance
  • Anger
  • Depression

 

Decided? Open to check: There is no correct order

It was a trick question. Elisabeth Kübler-Ross described these five responses in 1969, from interviews with people who were dying. She did not claim that people pass through them in sequence, and in 2005 she wrote that they are not stops on a linear timeline. People move between them, return to one they seemed to have left, and some never show one or more of them at all.

  • If you ranked them denial first and acceptance last, you hold the version that is usually taught, and the one families usually bring with them. Expect to meet it on placement.
  • A client who is angry in week one and angry again in week six has not gone backwards.
  • The five describe responses you may see. They are not a checklist of progress.

  • Main subject/content: How five responses became five steps. A vertical timeline. Events run from earliest at the top to latest at the bottom, joined by a single vertical line.

    • 1969: On Death and Dying is published. Five responses observed in people who were facing their own death.
    • 1970s and 1980s: Borrowed for bereavement, flattened into steps. Applied to grieving someone else's death, and taught as a sequence.
    • 2005: Kübler-Ross says they are not a sequence. On Grief and Grieving: not stops on a linear timeline, and not everyone experiences all of them.
